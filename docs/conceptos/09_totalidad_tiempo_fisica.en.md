# 9. Totality, time, and physical scope

## Totality as a contextual limit

In v0.72, T names unity without certifiable differences. For nonempty X and an empty contract, the quotient has one class. This is a consequence of the defined equivalence, not a demonstration that all physical relations disappear.

The emptiness \(\bot\) of a contradiction has zero realizations. T and \(\bot\) are not the same object. Preserving this difference matters when connecting the ontological language with SAT.

## Three ways to lose a visible difference

- Removing comparisons merges classes in a coarser description.
- Restricting realizations removes alternatives that are physically or logically admissible in the model.
- Synchronizing states changes their values under a dynamics.

The descriptive effects may look similar, but they are not interchangeable. Removing a redundancy does not change the quotient; removing a discriminating comparison can change it.

## Confluence and termination

If different elimination sequences end in the same family of observables, their final extensional quotient coincides. This does not prove termination of all dynamics or universal physical convergence.

Version 0.75 gives a concrete warning: on a circular phase space, the gradient can be zero while the defect is positive. Descending a function does not guarantee synchronization from every state.

## Relational time

The index s organizes updates. A temporal observable can be defined through phase differences or ratios of rates:

\[
\Delta_{AB}=\theta_A-\theta_B,\qquad
\rho_{A|B}=\frac{d\theta_A}{d\theta_B},
\]

where defined. Under a regular common reparameterization, the derivative ratio cancels the factor introduced by changing the parameter. This alone does not fix physical units, relativistic causality, or a law of evolution.

Without a comparable reference or record, there is no internal clock in this description. That is not equivalent to asserting zero physical frequency, that the universe stops, or that absolute simultaneity exists.

## Physical correspondences

The program explores matter as persistence of closures, particles as resonant modules, and gravity as relational integration. The sources distinguish these proposals from experimental results.

The relation \(R_V=4\hbar/(m_pc)\) appears as a historical correspondence in v0.78. This edition does not update constants or compare against external measurements: the attached corpus does not include a complete experimental evaluation here. An operational length is not automatically identified with a radius extracted from a form factor.

For physical progress, one needs a defined observable, a coupling rule, dimensional scales, and a prediction that could fail without retrospectively adjusting the architecture.
