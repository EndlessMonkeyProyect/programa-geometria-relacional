# Request for independent review: mathematical correspondences in the Riemann–Alpöge–Claude dossier

The incorporation of successive arithmetic references through dilation produces a family of functional approximation spaces in which inheritance, recovery, redundancy and cost can be expressed mathematically. The accompanying technical note develops this construction and compares its objects with those appearing in the Alpöge–Claude argument.

The prime-generated and squarefree reference chains constitute instances of the Báez-Duarte criterion. The dossier derives the corresponding projection-gain identities, Mellin–Parseval representation, Gram determinant and covolume formulas, and a lower bound on the scale required to attain a prescribed precision. The centred von Mangoldt observable described in item 5 of Alpöge's *Remark* provides a shared classical arithmetic interface.

Independent mathematical examination is invited at the level of both results and points of view: the exact identities, the convergence equivalences, the inheritance of arithmetic references, and the possible transfer of estimates between these constructions.

## Specific questions for review

1. **Arithmetic interface (§§3.1–3.10).** Confirm the sieve update, Mellin identities, explicit formula for W(X), the analytic equivalence with RH, and the identification of the observable in item 5 of Alpöge's *Remark*. Examine the domains, centering, and classical references.
2. **Functional equivalence (§§4.3–4.7).** Examine the prime-generated and squarefree chains as instances of Báez-Duarte, including the Möbius-supported approximants and the O58 coverage argument. Distinguish convergence equivalence from quantitative comparability at a fixed scale (L1).
3. **Minimum economy (§§4.4–4.8).** Examine exact projection gain, information in the isonormal Gaussian model, the scale bound, and the distinction between cardinality, generation cost, angular redundancy and directional recovery.
4. **Transfer between mechanisms (Problem T; §§4.6–4.8, 7, 9).** Identify whether theta-lift multiplicity, arithmetic divisibility, norms, covolumes and mean slopes in the reconstruction of (M), especially H7 and H8, admit a map or inequality connecting them to the relational quantities. A preserved invariant, a direct estimate, or a counterexample would each provide a precise mathematical outcome.
5. **Formal comparison and provenance (§§4.1–4.2, 5, 9).** Examine the reported formal statements and dependencies separately from the classical reconstruction. Identify relevant earlier literature and documentary records, with exact formulations and public availability evidence where applicable.

The full note provides the twenty-item comparison matrix and the review tasks A–H, with criteria of success and contrast. Corrections, alternative formulations, counterexamples, and bibliographic references are welcome. Individual sections or questions may be examined separately.
