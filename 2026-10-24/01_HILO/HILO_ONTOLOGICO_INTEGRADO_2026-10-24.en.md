# I. Language

## 1. What is real is what can be described [O]

Human nature is not written only in the genetic code. It also has an immaterial part, which is language. Through selection, language develops a structure that seeks the greatest efficiency in describing reality, and what is real is what can be described.

That structure is inherited, grows and is completed as part of a collective entity. We are the flowers and fruits of an organism whose materiality projects beyond biology and beyond what genetic information contains. Each of us is like one fruit of the tree, or like a mushroom whose spore is the mother structure: an individual projection of a collective knowledge. The difference that gives us identity is the one that keeps novelty alive, so that we can grow beyond the present.

## 2. The thread [O]

There is a thread. We do not see it whole, but we hold it. It is the continuity between what we distinguished yesterday and what we distinguish today, and that is our construction of reality. The relations in that construction need an ontological representation.

## 3. To reflect [O]

To reflect is to notice oneself within a larger circumstance: the proprioception of a system inside something that contains it. Reflection does not depend on language; language names it and keeps it. That is why it is the most fundamental layer of the program.

Intuition remains a legitimate way of understanding, because reflection is first seen in oneself.

## 4. Rule R: a relation exists if it can be named [D]

In the program, a relation exists only if it can be named within the language.

Formally, a language groups realizations into classes, and its reality is the quotient Ω/∼. A relation ρ is representable in that language if it respects the classes: if x ∼ x′ and y ∼ y′, then ρ(x, y) ⇔ ρ(x′, y′). If it does not, deciding it requires refining the language: that is a **novelty**.

The same rule reappears in the three branches (Part VI): a question that the previous signature cannot answer, a phase difference that magnitudes do not see, a commutator that the product erases. There it is a consequence of the thread, not its foundation.

## 5. Counting is the simplest case of language [O · T]

The simplest case of language is counting. The question is which numbers a reference can name when all it has is its first distinction, two, and how that vocabulary grows.

Each prime is a new word. It enters at the step n at which, counting with that prime, doubling n times returns to one for the first time. After the first step, every step brings a new word except one: the 6 ‹3›.

This is "The thread of two", recovered in full in `fuentes/06_hilo_del_dos/`.

## 6. The economy of a word [D]

A new word has to do identifiable work: keep an answer, distinguish cases, reduce a declared cost or produce a prediction that can be tested. If a new name changes no operation, it is not progress.

Language can also erase. A formulation that groups as equal some cases that an admitted operation distinguishes loses information; R‑02 has an exact witness of this (Part VI, Yang–Mills).

What is resolved does not become unresolved. A new question can separate what an earlier answer grouped together, without making that answer false for its own question.

Every proposal in the program is written in five voices:
1. a faithful narrative;
2. a typed definition;
3. a verifiable proposition;
4. an implementation;
5. a falsifier.

# II. The thread [O]

**1. The totality.** At the beginning there is one: homogeneous, without distinction. Its property is indeterminacy, an open difference with neither magnitude nor direction. The smallest form of that indeterminacy is a bit: plus or minus one, without being able to say which.

**2. Reflection.** The first operation is a mirror: noticing oneself within something larger. As soon as the unit stops being only one, there are two things at once: what is distinguished, and that from which it is distinguished.

**3. The cascade.** Reflection repeats in every reflection, and at each step its solutions emerge together, at the same time. Each node reflects the unit again as a ±1, and around each center N its images N − 1 and N + 1 appear: around 4, the 3 and the 5; around 8, the 7 and the 9.

Doubling gives depth: 2, 4, 8, 16. Squaring gives reclosure: 2, 4, 16, 256; at each reclosure everything before fits again as a single unit. The 3 is not assumed: it appears with the 4, as the comparisons that two distinctions allow.

**4. Normalizing the cardinals.** From inside, the cell always measures one. From outside, as the wall moves, each cell is normalized to the totality as a fraction 1/k. When a new multiple appears, a relation that before had nothing to be compared with, everything else is built on it. Two numbers with no common divisor are perpendicular: independent axes, because there is always a remainder.

**5. Opening or closing.** To close is to have self-referential information: to return to itself after a finite number of steps without losing the relational structure. What decides whether a configuration closes is normalization:
- if the fractions exceed the totality, it closes;
- if they fill it exactly, it repeats;
- if they fall short, it opens.

Angle and identity are the same thing: two mirrors meeting at an angle π/k is the same as applying them k times in a row being doing nothing.

**6. Even and odd.** What is even closes with its opposite; what is odd stays open.

**7. The 6.** The 6 is the multiplicity of the 2 and the 3, repeating. A half and a third leave exactly a sixth: the 6 fills the totality without bringing anything new, and that is why the configuration repeats there. In it a closed part, that of the 2, lives together with an open part, that of the 3. It is the same mechanism that makes the 6 the only step of the genealogy without a new word.

**8. The 5 and the undefined one.** When the one becomes undefined, the golden ratio appears: the number that is one plus its own inverse, the fraction made only of ones that never ends. It is the rectangle which, when a square is removed, leaves another with the same shape as the whole: what remains after distinguishing recognizes itself in the whole.

The golden ratio belongs to the same family as reflection, that of the square minus one. The 5 is a sphere around the 4: it closes only that way, by enveloping, and it cannot repeat on a flat surface.

**9. Background and islands.** The structure on which everything grows is that of the octaves, that of the indeterminacy of the tetrahedron. The tetrahedron is the background that never closes: each reflection brings something new. Space is in 8 and keeps moving; particles are in 4. On that background, projections appear that do close, such as the cube, and each projection is a configuration that can occur.

**10. The singularity.** A configuration that defines itself is a singularity: it holds itself up in that space. Its indefiniteness does not surround it and is not a content of it: it is its identity, its own information. Each singularity has its own.

**11. The seed.** In each closure a seed of the multiplicity remains, with the shape of the whole, as a reference from which to emerge again. Scale remains as the normalizer.

**12. The branches.** Navier–Stokes, P versus NP and Yang–Mills are three places where this same pair, opening and closing, reappears (Part VI).

# IV. Formal core

The minimum that holds the thread up, with its status. The proofs are in the files cited in Part IX.

1. **What is distinguished and what remains undistinguished.** Let C be a set of compatible realizations and Q a family of questions. The realizations fall into classes F_k of weight μ_k = |F_k|/|C|, and then:
   - Res = Σ μ_k log₂(1/μ_k) is what the language already distinguishes.
   - Ind = Σ μ_k log₂|F_k| is what remains undistinguished within each class.
   - **Res + Ind = log₂|C|** [T ‹1›]: the totality does not change; its distribution does.

   K_Q = |C/∼_Q| counts the classes: 0 is inconsistency, 1 is resolution, and more than 1 is sustained indeterminacy [D].

2. **Reflection about a center.** M_N(d) = (N − d, N + d), and N² − (N − d)(N + d) = d² [T]. With d = 1, the two images rebuild the center up to one unit: 3 · 5 = 16 − 1 and 7 · 9 = 64 − 1.

3. **Genealogy.** The prime p enters at the step n at which doubling n times returns to one modulo p for the first time: ord_p(2) = n.
   - Every n brings a prime of its own, except n = 1 and n = 6 ‹3›.
   - That is why the center 8 is the only one with no new word above it: 7 · 9 = 63 = 2⁶ − 1, and 9 = 3² is inherited [T].

4. **Reclosure.** The chain 2, 4, 16, 256, 65536 squares at each step. Each step closes the previous one and brings a novelty coprime to everything that was there ‹4› [T].

5. **Novelty, common reference and normalization.** This is R‑01 (`../03_RAMAS/yang_mills/R01/`), T1–T4.
   - If the previous signature is x mod N and a prime p appears that does not divide N, the previous signature cannot answer the question modulo p (T1) [T].
   - The common reference L = Np recovers both signatures, and each previous class splits into exactly p new classes (T2 ‹2›) [T].
   - The relation is preserved: j/N = pj/L. In general, if h/N passes to a multiple L, (hL)/(NL) = h/N, even though the numerator and the representation change (T3) [T].
   - With affine continuations, each part updates from its own residue without knowing the whole: an operational closure (T4) [T].
   - Written falsifiers [F]:
     - a constant map preserves commutation and erases every difference;
     - going from 6 to 18 with the 9 refines without bringing a new prime, because it deepens the 3;
     - the least common multiple gives representability, not dynamics.
   - The agreed sequence [D] is: novelty, independent multiple, internal normalization, common reference, own closure, continuity.

6. **Angle and identity.** Two mirrors meet at the angle π/k if and only if (s_i s_j)^k = 1 [T ‹5›]. With three mirrors at angles π/p, π/q and π/r ‹5›:
   - if 1/p + 1/q + 1/r > 1, the enclosure closes;
   - if it equals 1, it repeats;
   - if it is less than 1, it opens.

   CME‑002 checks this on 22 enclosures [T · E].

7. **Interface.** Γ_G(B) = ∃I R_G(I, B) replaces the interior by its relation with the boundary B [D]. Adding constraints never enlarges what is compatible: Ω_{n+1} = Ω_n ∩ R_{n+1} [T]. Contextual singularity: Ω ≠ ∅ and |Q(Ω)| = 1, which does not require |Ω| = 1 [D].

8. **Dynamical sufficiency.** A signature Q is sufficient for an evolution F if Q ∘ F = F̂ ∘ Q. Across scales, the signature is preserved if Q = P ∘ Q′ ∘ J [D · T in R‑02].

9. **Complement.** ‖V‖² + ‖m‖² = 1 [D]. With E = ħω_U, pc = ħω_V and mc² = ħω_m, this is exactly the relativistic relation between energy, momentum and mass [T].
